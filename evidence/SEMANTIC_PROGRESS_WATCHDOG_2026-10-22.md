This is an operating-control milestone, not a commercial outcome.

## Standing C-suite learning doctrine

> **Every failure needs to become a lesson for the C-suite, improving policy every time.**

Operationally, this means:

- terminal failures remain immutable evidence rather than being rewritten as success;
- the CEO/C-suite classifies the failure and extracts its causal lesson;
- policy, selector logic, verifier behavior, process constraints or acceptance criteria are hardened when the evidence warrants it;
- repeated failure of the same effective class without learning is itself a stagnation signal;
- failed work is not silently retried under renamed identities and failure never manufactures new authority;
- learning must survive model/session replacement because canonical MetaBot evidence and policy, not Hermes chat history, are authoritative;
- one-off execution errors must be distinguished from systemic policy/runtime gaps so the company does not overfit a single failure into an unsafe global rule.

Learning remains subordinate to governance: the company may improve execution, but it may not expand its own authority.

## Evidence boundary

This milestone does **not** establish:

- a new qualified buyer;
- a customer;
- revenue;
- collected cash;
- completed fulfillment;
- delivery;
- end-to-end commercial success.

Those require separate receipts and independent verification.

## Why this matters

A process watchdog asks whether the CEO is alive. A loop watchdog asks whether cycles complete. The semantic progress watchdog asks whether bounded execution is actually reducing uncertainty, changing company state, advancing an opportunity, repairing a blocker or producing another verified economic transition.

That distinction turns failure into institutional learning rather than repeated activity.
