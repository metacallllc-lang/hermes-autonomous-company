# Digital workforce

## The worker is a business role

A digital worker should be designed around:

- responsibility;
- inputs;
- tools;
- outputs;
- success criteria;
- authority;
- checkpoints;
- escalation;
- evidence.

That is different from simply giving a chatbot a job title.

## Examples

Potential digital roles include:

- sales development;
- sales operations;
- research;
- customer support;
- bookkeeping operations;
- accounts receivable;
- reporting;
- recruiting coordination;
- content and SEO;
- operations support.

## Coordinated workforce

The higher-value product is not a bag of agents.

It is a coordinated workforce:

```text
CEO / human leadership
       │
       ├── Sales
       │    ├── Sales Development
       │    └── Sales Operations
       │
       ├── Marketing
       │    └── Content / SEO
       │
       ├── Finance
       │    └── Finance Operations
       │
       └── Support
            └── Help Desk
```

## Human checkpoints

A well-designed worker has explicit boundaries.

Examples:

- drafts rather than signs a legal commitment;
- prepares a payment rather than bypassing payment authority;
- researches prospects without inventing private facts;
- queues publication only to the approved brand identity;
- escalates when required evidence is missing.

## Ongoing operation

Digital workforces require operations:

- monitoring;
- maintenance;
- model changes;
- integration updates;
- bug repair;
- prompt / workflow tuning;
- performance measurement;
- governance updates.

That recurring operational layer is part of the commercial product.
